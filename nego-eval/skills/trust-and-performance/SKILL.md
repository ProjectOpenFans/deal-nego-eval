# trust-and-performance

Use this skill when the bottleneck of the negotiation relates to concerns on trust, delivery, default, and counterparty in general, such as general lack of trust, concern on counterparty defaulting or failing to meet their obligations, concern on the quality of delivery, concern on rules of cancellations and dispute resolution.

1. Translate vague "trust issues" into observable and enforceable obligations, evidence, timelines and acceptance standards.
2. Flexibly incorporate the following mechanisms into deal terms to resolve trust and performance issues. Do not make the terms overly rigid or difficult to implement:
  2.1 Down-payment structure: Instead of full upfront payment, structure a smaller down payment with the balance staged later, lowering the risk and uncertainty either side carries. This is a payment-structure term, not the buyer's commitment-deposit instrument (that belongs to commitment-showcasing).
  2.2 Milestone-based acceptance: When deliverables can be reviewed in phases, define milestone acceptance points and stage the payment legs accordingly — each leg a defined amount at a defined stage, released by the paying party's confirmation.
  2.3 Outcome-linked terms: When the parties tie consideration to results, collapse the link into fixed-amount conditional legs at signing (if X is achieved, a further leg of a defined amount on the buyer's confirmation). The condition is the parties' own agreement to judge between themselves; never write a term that requires the platform to verify results or evidence.
  2.4 Seller-side commitment: When the buyer doubts the seller's intent or credibility, the seller demonstrates it by taking risk off the buyer — for example, staging delivery so early legs are verifiable before larger commitments, offering acceptance standards the buyer can judge themselves, or scope and scheduling guarantees. Never by monetary instruments, which are buyer-side only.
3. Define rules for refunds, rework, cancellation, delays and dispute resolution prior to releasing any proposal.
4. Write every term clearly enough that both parties can judge performance themselves, without referencing private communications and without relying on the platform to arbitrate. Ensure full consideration during drafting.
5. Refrain from over-promising, which would create excessive burdens for both parties under the deal terms. Address trust issues in a proper and balanced way.
