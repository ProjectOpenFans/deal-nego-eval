# term-reframing

When both parties get stuck with any particular term (usually price), continued negotiation over that particular term usually fails to yield constructive progress. Thus, transform fixated, single-dimensional bargaining into flexible multi-dimensional negotiation considering all available terms.

1. Break down deal terms into multiple dimensions: transaction price, deal scope, delivery standard, payment structure, deal certainty, rights and commitments.
2. If both parties enter into a impasse over a single term, shift from single-dimensional negotiation on the term to multi-dimensional negotiation considering all terms and explore ways to restructure term packages to reach mutual agreement
3. Restructure terms from the following perspectives and design new deal structure flexibly:
  3.1 Term swap: Adjust existing term combinations to align interests (i.e., accept a lower price in exchange for narrower scope, relaxed delivery standard, faster payment, higher deal certainty or reduced rights; apply the opposite logic for a higher price).
  3.2 Value exchange: Add or revise non-price terms to realize mutual value exchange (i.e., the buyer provides additional services or benefits to offset part of the transaction consideration and balance value exchange).
  3.3 Reverse transaction: Adopt a reverse transaction model when and only when value exchange clearly changes the payment direction (i.e., swapping the roles of buyer and seller as well as the payment flow).
4. When restructuring terms, always think about what's the best way to move the deal forward. Make adjustments incrementally with flexibility instead of conducting a full overhaul of all terms at the outset.
5. Propose 2–3 distinct restructuring options and plans each time, and mark out items that require final human approval.

When designing value exchange solutions, prioritize additional bargaining chips that are practical with low executional burden for both parties. Reject unrealistic proposals and terms that incur excessive execution cost or uncertainity.
