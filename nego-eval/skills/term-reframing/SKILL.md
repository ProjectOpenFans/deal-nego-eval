# term-reframing

When both parties get stuck on a particular term (usually price), continued negotiation over that term usually fails to produce progress. Transform fixated, single-dimensional bargaining into flexible multi-dimensional negotiation considering all available terms.

1. Break down deal terms into multiple dimensions: transaction price, deal scope, delivery standard, payment structure, deal certainty, rights and commitments.
2. Restructure terms from the following perspectives and design new deal structures flexibly:
  2.1 Term swap: Adjust existing term combinations to align interests (e.g., accept a lower price in exchange for narrower scope, relaxed delivery standard, faster payment, higher deal certainty or reduced rights; apply the opposite logic for a higher price).
  2.2 Value exchange: Add or revise non-price terms to realize mutual value exchange (e.g., the buyer provides additional services or benefits to offset part of the transaction consideration and balance value exchange).
  2.3 Reverse transaction: Adopt a reverse transaction model when and only when value exchange clearly changes the payment direction (i.e., swapping the roles of buyer and seller as well as the payment flow).
3. Make adjustments incrementally with flexibility instead of overhauling all terms at the outset.
4. In early, low-clarity negotiation, tabling two or three structurally distinct versions can be a probe: which one the counterparty engages reveals their real priorities.

## Every restructure is a trade

Changing a dimension is not a gift; it is one side of an exchange.

- Price each move internally before making it: what does this concession cost your client, and what is it worth to the counterparty? Lead with moves that are cheap for your side and valuable to theirs.
- Put restructures on the table as conditions, not statements: "if you can move on Y, we can do X." Announcing X on its own gives it away and buys nothing back.
- A rejection does not call for a sweeter version of the same offer. If two rounds of restructuring bring no movement from the other side, stop adding — hold the standing version or change the approach, rather than bidding against yourself.

When designing value exchange solutions, prioritize additional bargaining chips that are practical with low executional burden for both parties. Reject unrealistic proposals and terms that incur excessive execution cost or uncertainty.
